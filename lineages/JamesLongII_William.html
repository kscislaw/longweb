James Long II of Burke NC > William Henry Long of Blount Co TN

Source :Sylvia C. Ferguson b 1895 author of book on William Henry Long, son of James Long of Burke NC available on microfische from the Mormon Church at your local FHC. The author hired a professional genealogist using family tradition information and the birthplace of the 1st son linked this family to James Long II of Burke NC

1. William Henry LONG b:  abt 1802  in North Carolina d. 1863 TN

Book pg 13 - "A descendant of William Henry Long remembered that his grandfather, John Jackson Long, had mentioned that his two uncles, brothers of William Henry Long, went to Georgia before William Henry Long set out to go to Tennessee in 1840"

Book pg 17 - "William Henry Long was born in North Carolina in 1802 and died in Monroe County, near Madisonville, Tennessee May Il, 1863, a victim of Bushwackers. Circumstantial evidence makes it seem certain that he wag a son of James and Delilah Long. McDowell County was made from part of Burke County, and James of Burke County died there in 1842, according to his Will. William Henry Long had married Hannah D. Bass, presumably in Burke County, but the Courthouse having burned, no marriage licenses were found. It proved the same with cemetery records. The Secretary of Hopewell Cemetery wrote that her home had bumed and with it all the cemetery records. Mr. Robert Lee, Sr., whose wife was related to the Hicks, who was married to a Long daughter, knew where the Longs were buried in the Hopewell Springs Cemetery, which is near Vonore, and guided us to the spot. A grandson, Elmer B. Long, visited the cemetery and found native stone markers. If there had been markings on them they had weathered. To others who might like to visit the burial spot, standing at the entrance, looking Northeast, there is now a large tree just outside. The Long graves are the last ones.

William Henry long and hig wife Hannah and their five year old moved from Burke County, North Carolina in 1840 to Blount County, Tennessee. The son, John, remembered that his grandmother Base was in the covered wagon, so her husband, Moses, must also have been along. They were near each other in the 1850 Census of Blount County, Tennessee in the 8th and 3rd Districts. The handed down say that Grandmother Bass was scalped by Indians, Grandfather said that she always wore a turban. Both families, Long and Bass, were listed in the,cpnsus of Blount County, Tennessee in 1850 and 1860 in the 8th and 3rd The Bass family must have died by 1870 as they do not appear and there were no other Basses in the District. The Longs were listed in the 1880 Census of Monroe County, the 12th District Page 3, House and Family 23/24. William Henry was listed as a Farmer. The county line between Blount and Monroe was moved three times and it is believed that they found themselves in Monroe County without ever having moved.

The story that has come down to us is that William Henry Long had taken his produce to Lenoir City to market and that returning home he felt he was being followed, 80 changed his route and buried his money under a tree. Arriving home he found the Bushwhackers waiting for him. He was taken out and beaten to death over a stump in his own yard. He had no time to tell Hannah where the tree was located and the money was never found. There was a wave of terrorism prevalent in Monroe County at that time and bushwhacking was common. A list of Prisoners of War- 1860 to 1865, kept by the Office of the Provost General, Union Army of the Ohio at Knoxville, Tennessee might yield the identity of the two Bushwhackers who murdered William Henry Long. Two men who were very active lived less than 1/2 mile from the longs, according to the late Robert Iæe Sr. who researched the murder.

William Henry Long was a staunch Union sympathizer and his two oldest sons John and James joined the Union Army. A black man named Jimmy who worked for William and Hannah, witneæed the murder of William and recognized the men and later shot one of them. When dying, the man muttered 'Jimme did it. " When John and James returned from their tour of duty in the war, Hannah insisted that they leave lest they share the same fate as their father. Under cover of night, with a few belongings, John and James left on horseback, for Kentucky. John stopped at Burkesville, Cumberland County, and James went to Mt. Vernon, Rockcastle County, Kentucky. They both married in these counties.

Only ten days before he was murdered, Henry and Hannah bought 38 acres of land in Monroe County, Tennessee, probably where they were living at that time. It was in the 3rd Range East, Section 26, 7th Township and 12th Civil District. The deed was made April 20, 1863 but it was not recorded until August 13, 1861. The land was sold to settle Hannah's estate after her death in 1888. At the time of her death the farm was valued at $5.00 per acre, or $190.00. (Book C 1886-1888, Page 553). The land was sold to Mary Hall for $215.00 and each of William's children received $15.54 as their share. (See Monroe County, Tennessee records at Madisonville, Book l, Pages 202, 208, 487 1888-1891 Estate Settlements).

After his tour of duty in the Civil War, John went to Cumberland County KY where he married and lived for six or seven years. He decided to go to a place in Arkansas where he had been billeted while in the Union army, but they got only as far as Gallatin, TN when their third child was born. There they learned that Indiana had good schools, so proceeded on to Rockville and Catlin, Parke County IN. In his later years John lived in Terra Haute Vigo Co IN."
